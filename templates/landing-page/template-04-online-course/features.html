The curriculum, module by module

Inkwell teaches the unglamorous machinery of the personal essay. Here is every module, how the cohort runs, and what you leave with.

See tuition Ask a question

The eight modules

Each module is one craft problem, solved

Not lectures — exercises that put the lesson straight onto your page.

Finding the true subject

Your topic is not your subject. The interview-yourself technique locates what the essay is really about, so you stop circling and start writing the thing that matters.

  • The topic-vs-subject test
  • Interview-yourself prompts
  • A one-line essay premise

Openings that owe nothing

Six proven first-page shapes, and a simple test that tells you which one your material has already chosen. End the week with three openings you would actually keep.

  • Six first-page shapes
  • The opening test
  • Three keepable drafts

Scene vs. summary

The braid every essay weaves: when to slow into scene, when to glide through summary, and how to make the seams disappear. The skill readers feel but never see.

  • When to scene, when to glide
  • Seam-smoothing drills
  • A fully braided passage

Writing the hard thing

Telling the truth about real people, including yourself, without cruelty and without flinching into vagueness. The module alumni say changed how they write.

  • Honesty without cruelty
  • The vagueness trap
  • Consent and care notes

Structure as argument

Chronology, collage, frame — each structure secretly argues something. Learn to pick the one your essay needs instead of defaulting to first-this-then-that.

  • Three structures decoded
  • What each one argues
  • Re-outline your draft

Revision in passes

The four-pass method — argument, architecture, line, breath. You will never again 'just read through' a draft and hope. Alumni call this alone worth tuition.

  • Four named passes
  • A revision checklist
  • One essay, fully revised

How the cohort works

Eight weeks, three essays, one editor's letter

Designed for people with jobs — expect four focused hours a week.

1

Watch + read

Each Monday unlocks a 40-minute craft lesson and two annotated exemplar essays.

2

Write to a brief

Weekly prompts build toward complete essays — never busywork or word-count theater.

3

Workshop live

Small-group critique every Thursday, kind and honest, capped at eight writers.

4

Get the letter

Finish with a written editor's letter on your strongest essay and a matched pitch list.
