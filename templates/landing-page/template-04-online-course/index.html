A letter from your instructor

You do not have a discipline problem. You have a draft problem.

Every writer I have taught arrived believing they lacked willpower. They had notebooks of openings, a folder of abandoned drafts, and a quiet suspicion that real writers feel certain. They do not. The blank page is not asking for certainty — it is asking for a process.

Advice culture makes this worse. Write every day, they say, as if frequency were structure. You can show up daily for a year and still circle the same three paragraphs if nobody teaches you what a draft is actually for.

⁂

A studio method, taught in eight honest modules.

Inkwell teaches the unglamorous machinery: how to find the true subject hiding under your topic, how to build a scene that earns its reflection, how to revise in passes instead of spirals — and how to take an edit without taking it personally.

You will write three full essays during the course. One of them — the one you are most afraid of — is usually the one our editors flag for publication. That is not a coincidence. That is the method.

Curriculum

The eight modules

Each module is one craft problem, solved on the page.

01

Finding the true subject

Why your topic is not your subject, and the interview-yourself technique that locates what the essay is really about.

02

Openings that owe nothing

Six proven first-page shapes — and the test that tells you which one your material has already chosen.

03

Scene vs. summary

The braid every essay weaves: when to slow into scene, when to glide, and how to feel the seams disappear.

04

Writing the hard thing

Telling the truth about real people, including yourself, without cruelty and without flinching into vagueness.

05

Structure as argument

Chronology, collage, frame: what each structure secretly argues, and how to pick the one your essay needs.

06

Revision in passes

The four-pass method: argument, architecture, line, breath. You will never again 'just read through' a draft.

07

Voice under pressure

Exercises that separate your written voice from your spoken one — then put the best of both on the page.

08

Publishing without panic

Where personal essays actually get read in 2026, how to pitch them, and how to survive the comments.
