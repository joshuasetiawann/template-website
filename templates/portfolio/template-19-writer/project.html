“The Last Ferry Schedule” — essay

8,000 words on island commuters and the timetable that holds a community together. Anthologised twice.

Overview

Overview

An essay about the last ferry timetable of a dying island route — and what schedules mean to places that are running out of them.

Reported across three crossings and forty years of timetable archives; the essay reads the route’s decline in minutes shaved and sailings dropped.

The brief

The challenge

The temptation was elegy. The essay had to stay precise — grief in the data, not in the adjectives.

Method

The approach

  1. 01

    Notice & gather

    Notebooks, ferry timetables, overheard menus. Essays begin as evidence collected long before the argument shows up.

  2. 02

    Find the argument

    The subject is never the subject. Drafting continues until the piece confesses what it is actually about.

  3. 03

    Draft & cut

    Long first drafts, then the cull: every paragraph must either advance the thought or earn its keep as music.

  4. 04

    Edit aloud, then let go

    Read aloud, fact-check, and stop fiddling. A piece is finished when touching it makes it worse.
