The longer story

A bit more about me

I write essays, reportage and the occasional book. Thirteen years at the desk taught me that the work is mostly noticing — paying closer attention than is strictly comfortable, then finding the sentence that makes a reader notice too.

My subjects keep returning to cities, food and the small machinery of ordinary life: a ferry schedule, a night market, the politics of an open-plan office. I am drawn to the overlooked, because that is where the surprising true things hide.

I report before I write. Even the essays start with interviews, walking and reading — the opinion is only worth having once it has survived contact with the facts.

I edit until it disappears. The goal is prose a reader falls through without noticing the craft, then looks up changed. That takes more drafts than anyone wants to admit.
