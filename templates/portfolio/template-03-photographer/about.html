The longer story

A bit more about me

I make photographs slowly. Fifteen years in, I still believe the best frames come from staying somewhere long after the obvious picture has been taken — the second week, the bad weather, the moment everyone relaxes. Trust is the lens I shoot with most.

My practice splits between long documentary essays and editorial commissions. The essays — salt harvesters, night-shift workers, a single alpine ridge across a year — are where I learn. The commissions are where that patience pays a client back, on a deadline, in a usable edit.

I shoot mostly medium-format film and print large. The grain, the slowness, the cost of every frame — these are features, not nostalgia. They make me wait for the picture instead of taking forty and hoping.

I edit hard. A forty-image essay usually starts as three thousand frames. The work people see is the work I was willing to cut everything else for.
