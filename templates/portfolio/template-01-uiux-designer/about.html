The longer story

A bit more about me

My route into design was sideways: a psychology degree, a stint in user support, and a growing irritation that good research kept dying in slide decks. I learned to prototype so the insight could survive contact with a roadmap. Eight years later that is still the job — turning what people actually do into interfaces a team can ship on a Thursday.

I work in systems, not screens. A button is never just a button; it is a decision about hierarchy, a token, a state machine, and a promise to the next designer who touches it. I like the unglamorous parts — empty states, error copy, the third edge case nobody scoped — because that is where products earn trust.

Most of my engagements start with a discovery sprint and end with an annotated handoff engineers genuinely like. In between there is a lot of greyscale, a lot of moderated testing, and a stubborn refusal to add a setting where a better default would do.

I care about accessibility as craft, not compliance. WCAG is the floor. The work is making the calm, legible, keyboard-first version also the most beautiful one — so nobody has to choose.
