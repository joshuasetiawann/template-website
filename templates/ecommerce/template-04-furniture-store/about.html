Our story

How we got here

Grain & Form grew out of a one-bench workshop in Vermont in 2014, where a furniture maker who was tired of flat-pack particleboard started building chairs the way his grandfather had — mortise and tenon, hand-planed, finished in oil you could smell across the room.

We work in solid hardwood, almost never veneer. Every board is selected for its figure, milled in our own shop, and joined with traditional joinery that gets stronger with age. A glued panel fails at the glue line; a dovetail only tightens. We build for the second generation, not the first delivery.

Our timber is FSC-certified and, wherever we can, locally felled — storm oak, urban ash, reclaimed barn beams that already have a century of weather in them. We finish in plant-based oils and natural wax, never spray lacquer, so a scratch can be sanded out at home rather than sent back.

We sell direct and make largely to order, which keeps our forest footprint honest and our warehouse small. A Grain & Form chair takes longer to arrive because someone is actually making it — and it will still be in the family when the trend that inspired it is long forgotten.

How we work

Four things the workshop insists on

01

Joinery, not glue

Mortise, tenon and dovetail joints that tighten with age — built to outlive the people who buy them.

02

Solid wood only

FSC-certified hardwood, often locally felled. Veneer is the exception, never the rule.

03

Finishes you can mend

Plant-based oils and natural wax, so a mark can be sanded and re-oiled at home.

04

Made to order

We cut the tree only when there’s a home for the board — no speculative overproduction.

Timber & finish

On wood

We mill storm-felled and urban oak, ash and walnut, plus reclaimed beams with a century of character already in them. Every piece is dried in our own kiln, joined with hide glue and traditional joinery, and finished in hardwax oil with zero VOC spray. Offcuts become stools, cutting boards and kindling — a tree leaves our shop as furniture, not waste.
