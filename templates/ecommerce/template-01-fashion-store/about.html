The house behind the wardrobe

Maison Linnea began with a single question — what would a wardrobe look like if every piece were made to be kept? Two decades later, that question still cuts our patterns.

Our story

How we got here

We started in 2009 above a haberdashery in the second arrondissement, with one cutting table, a borrowed steam press and a stubborn belief that natural fibres age better than trends. Our first run was forty trench coats, sold to friends and the friends of friends, every one hemmed by hand on a Sunday.

Today the house is larger but the table is the same. Maison Linnea designs in Paris and produces in three small European workshops we have known for years — a knit house in the Scottish borders, a tailoring studio outside Florence, and a silk finisher in Lyon. We place small orders, we pay invoices early, and we keep the same hands on the same cloth season after season.

Considered womenswear, to us, means restraint. We would rather perfect a coat over two seasons than launch a dozen styles a month. Each garment earns its place through cut, drape and the way it feels at the end of a long day — not through a logo or a marketing calendar.

We make in small batches and we make to last. A Linnea coat is cut with seam allowances deep enough to let out, buttons chosen so they can be replaced, and a lining that can be relined. Quiet luxury, the unfashionable kind — the kind that is still in the wardrobe a decade on.

What we hold to

Cloth & craft

On materials

Every season opens with cloth, not sketches. We travel to the mills first — to Biella for wool, to Lyon for silk, to the Scottish borders for cashmere — and design around what the fibre wants to do. Offcuts go back to the knit house to be re-spun; our packaging is plastic-free, printed with soy ink and built to be reused as a garment bag. Nothing about a Linnea piece is designed to be thrown away.
