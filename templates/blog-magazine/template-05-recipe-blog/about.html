Our story

How this masthead came to be

Butter & Thyme started with a drawer of index cards splattered past legibility and a promise: never publish a recipe we hadn't cooked at least three times, including once when tired and hungry. That last test — the 7 p.m. on a Tuesday test — is where most recipes quietly fall apart, and where ours have to hold.

Every recipe here is developed in a real home kitchen, on a domestic oven that runs ten degrees hot like yours probably does. We weigh, we re-weigh, and we write down the failures so you don't have to repeat them. If a step matters, we tell you why; if it doesn't, we cut it.

We believe good cooking is mostly good defaults plus permission to deviate. So our recipes give you the reliable version and then the variations — the swap for what's in your fridge, the shortcut for a weeknight, the flourish for a Sunday. The Test Kitchen notes show the working.

No fourteen-paragraph life story before the ingredient list. The headnote tells you what the dish is, why it works, and what to watch for — then gets out of the way so you can cook.

Editorial mission

To publish recipes that actually work the first time — tested in a real kitchen, written without filler, and generous with the why.

How we work

Three rules we don't bend

The standards behind everything we publish.

01

Tested three times

Minimum. Once to develop, once to verify, and once at 7 p.m. on a Tuesday when nobody wants to cook. If it fails then, it fails.

02

Weighed, not guessed

We give grams as well as cups, because baking is chemistry and 'a handful' is not a measurement we can stand behind.

03

The why, briefly

If a step matters we explain it in a line. If it doesn't, we delete it. Your screen-time before dinner is sacred.
