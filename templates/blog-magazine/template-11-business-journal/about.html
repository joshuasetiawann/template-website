Our story

How this masthead came to be

The Ledger Standard was founded on the conviction that business journalism should answer to the balance sheet, not the press release. Too much of it reprints corporate narrative as analysis. We read the filing, check the footnotes, and tell you what the numbers say even when the company would rather you read the headline.

We cover Markets, Companies, Economy and Deals with reporters who can read a 10-K and a cap table. Our opinion pages are clearly separated from our reporting and clearly argued. We disclose conflicts, we don't trade on what we cover, and we correct in public when we're wrong.

Rigor is the house style. A markets piece cites the data series; a deal story names the terms and the risk; an economy column shows the model's assumptions. We are skeptical of consensus precisely because everyone repeating it is what makes it dangerous.

The Ledger Standard is independent and subscriber-funded. The Bell newsletter rings before the open with the few things that moved and the one number worth watching all day.

Editorial mission

To report business against the filing, not the press release — rigorous on markets, clear on conflicts, and skeptical of consensus.

How we work

Three rules we don't bend

The standards behind everything we publish.

01

Read the filing

We report against the 10-K and the footnotes, not the press release. The narrative is the company's job; the numbers are ours.

02

Wall off opinion

Reporting and opinion are clearly separated and clearly argued. We disclose conflicts and never trade on what we cover.

Cite the data

Markets pieces name the series, deal stories name the terms, columns show the assumptions. Rigor is the house style.
