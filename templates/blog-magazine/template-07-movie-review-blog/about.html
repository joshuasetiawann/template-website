Our story

How this masthead came to be

Grindhouse Weekly was born in a repertory cinema's back row, arguing about a film that everyone else had already filed away as minor. We started because the takes felt too safe — consensus delivered as criticism. A star rating should be the end of an argument, not a substitute for one.

We review across the spectrum: the prestige release and the straight-to-streaming oddity, the festival darling and the midnight cult object. A great B-movie gets the same close attention as an awards contender, because the craft is the craft whatever the budget. We are snobs only about effort.

Every review states its case. We tell you what the film is trying to do, whether it does it, and what it cost us to watch — in the best and worst sense. We disclose when we've seen a director's earlier work and when we're walking in cold. The rating is honest; the reasons are the point.

Grindhouse Weekly is independent and proudly opinionated. The Reel newsletter sends one passionate argument a week, no spoilers above the fold.

Editorial mission

To review films with conviction across every budget and genre — a star rating that ends an argument because it actually made one.

How we work

Three rules we don't bend

The standards behind everything we publish.

01

State the case

Every review argues a position: what the film attempts, whether it lands, and why the rating is what it is. No fence-sitting.

02

Every budget counts

A great B-movie earns the same scrutiny as an awards contender. We judge ambition and execution, not the line item.

03

Disclose the angle

We tell you when we know a director's earlier work and when we walk in cold, so you can weigh the verdict for yourself.
