Our story

How this masthead came to be

Frequency started as a zine traded at a basement show, photocopied and stapled at 2 a.m. The conviction then is the conviction now: write about music as if the band is in the room and the reader actually cares what something sounds like, not just whether it's cool.

We review albums across genres and refuse to grade on a curve. A debut from a band of teenagers gets the same close listen as a legacy act's tenth record. We report scenes from inside them, not from a press release, and we credit the engineers and the rooms as much as the names on the cover.

Our gear coverage is for people who care how the sound got made — the pedal, the preamp, the take that almost didn't happen. We've blind-tested setups, argued about reverb tails, and published the receipts. Snobbery about price is banned; snobbery about care is encouraged.

Frequency is independent and loud about it. The B-Side newsletter sends one record worth your week, plus the scene report that didn't fit anywhere else.

Editorial mission

To write about music with conviction and curiosity — albums graded honestly, scenes reported from inside, and the craft behind the sound credited.

How we work

Three rules we don't bend

The standards behind everything we publish.

01

No grading on a curve

A debut and a legacy record get the same close listen. We judge what's in the speakers, not the size of the name.

02

Scenes from inside

We report from the venue, not the press release — the bands, the bookers and the rooms that hold a sound together.

03

Credit the craft

Engineers, producers and the rooms themselves get named. The take that almost didn't happen is usually the story.
