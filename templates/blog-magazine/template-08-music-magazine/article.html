Moth Choir's 'Porchlight' Reviewed: Folk for the End of the Group Chat

Four-part harmonies, one busted tape machine, and the bravest 38 seconds of silence committed to vinyl this year.

DC

Deshaun Cole

Jun 4, 2026·7 min read

The record opens mid-thought, like you walked in on a conversation already in progress. Four-part harmonies, one busted tape machine, and the bravest 38 seconds of silence committed to vinyl this year.

What follows is less a verdict than a working argument, assembled from notes, false starts and one or two opinions I have since revised. The headline is up top; everything below it has to earn the claim or quietly take it back.

What the record is reaching for

The album opens mid-thought, as if you walked in on a conversation already underway, and never quite slows down to introduce itself. That is either a flaw or the entire point, depending on how much you trust an artist to know where they are going.

There is a restlessness in the production — a refusal to repeat a hook the easy way — that marks this as the work of a band bored of its own competence. When it connects, the songs feel discovered rather than written.

Not everything coheres. A couple of tracks chase an idea past the point of diminishing returns, and the sequencing buries one of the best songs in the back half. But a swing-and-a-miss from a band this ambitious is more interesting than a safe hit from a lesser one.

The tracks I keep coming back to

  • The opener, which establishes the whole grammar of the record in ninety seconds.
  • A mid-album ballad that strips everything back and dares you to look away.
  • The closer, which resolves a tension you did not realise the album had been holding.

Where it lands, and where it drifts

Be patient with the boring middle. Beginnings are theatrical and endings are quotable, but the middle is where the real decisions live, unglamorous and undocumented. The block below is the part I would clip and keep.

Three things to carry out the door

  • Start smaller than feels respectable; momentum is the only currency that compounds.
  • Write down the thing you are afraid to be wrong about. Revisit it in a month.
  • Borrow ruthlessly, attribute generously, and keep the version that survives contact with reality.

Put it on loud, start to finish, with the lights low and the phone in another room. This is a record built for immersion, and it gives back exactly as much attention as you are willing to pay it.
