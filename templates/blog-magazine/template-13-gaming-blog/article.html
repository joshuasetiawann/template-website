'Lanternfall' Is the Cozy Roguelike That Finally Cracked It

Death means a new village festival, not a run reset. The genre's kindest loop is also its cleverest.

ZM

Zaria Mensah

Jun 4, 2026·8 min read

I went in expecting a tech demo and came out three hours late for dinner. Death means a new village festival, not a run reset. The genre's kindest loop is also its cleverest.

What follows is less a verdict than a working argument, assembled from notes, false starts and one or two opinions I have since revised. The headline is up top; everything below it has to earn the claim or quietly take it back.

What the game gets right

I went in expecting a tech demo and lost an evening. The systems do not just coexist; they argue with each other in productive ways, and the best moments emerge from a designer trusting you to misuse their tools.

The opening hours are stingy, almost hostile, withholding the verbs that make the whole thing sing. Push past them. Once the systems unlock and start talking, the game stops being a series of tasks and becomes a place you keep finding reasons to stay.

It is not flawless. The menus fight you, the difficulty curve has a cliff in the middle act, and one mechanic never quite earns the screen time it demands. But the ambition on display makes the rough edges feel like the cost of someone actually trying something.

What hooked me, and what nearly lost me

  • Emergent moments the designers clearly did not script and obviously hoped for.
  • A world that rewards curiosity instead of punishing it with a loading screen.
  • An early grind that needs a patch and a tutorial that explains everything except the fun.

Where it tests your patience

Be patient with the boring middle. Beginnings are theatrical and endings are quotable, but the middle is where the real decisions live, unglamorous and undocumented. The block below is the part I would clip and keep.

bench.js
// the eval harness that kept us honest
async function bench(model, queries) {
  const hits = [];
  for (const q of queries) {
    const t0 = performance.now();
    const top = await model.retrieve(q.text, { k: 5 });
    hits.push({
      q: q.id,
      ms: performance.now() - t0,
      ok: top.some((d) => d.id === q.gold),
    });
  }
  const recall = hits.filter((h) => h.ok).length / hits.length;
  return { recall, p95: percentile(hits.map((h) => h.ms), 95) };
}

Give it the four hours it needs to become itself. Most reviews quit before the unlock, and most players who push past it stop checking the clock entirely. This is one of the latter.
