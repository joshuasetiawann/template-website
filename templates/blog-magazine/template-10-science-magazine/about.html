Our story

How this masthead came to be

Orbital exists because the gap between a paper and a headline is where most science journalism goes wrong. A result gets flattened into a claim, the uncertainty gets deleted, and the reader ends up more confident and less informed. We set out to do the opposite: explain the work, keep the caveats, and respect both the science and the curiosity that brings people to it.

We cover five fields — Space, Biology, Physics, Climate and Minds — with writers who read the methods section, not just the abstract. We talk to the researchers, and to researchers who weren't involved, because the most useful question is often 'what would convince you this is wrong?'

Every Orbital piece distinguishes what a study shows from what it suggests from what it merely hints. We explain effect sizes and error bars in plain language because they are the difference between news and noise. When the science is uncertain, we say so — uncertainty is not a flaw in the story, it is the story.

Orbital is independent and not for sale. The Briefing newsletter sends one genuinely new result a week, explained well enough that you could in turn explain it to someone else.

Editorial mission

To explain research faithfully — keeping the uncertainty, the methods and the meaning intact — for readers who are curious, not credulous.

How we work

Three rules we don't bend

The standards behind everything we publish.

01

Read the methods

We report from the methods section, not the press release. The how is where a result earns — or loses — its meaning.

02

Keep the uncertainty

Effect sizes and error bars stay in, in plain language. When the science is unsettled, we say so plainly.

03

Ask the critics

Every story checks with researchers who weren't involved. 'What would change your mind?' is our favourite question.
