Our story

How this masthead came to be

Aperture Null is a zine about looking. It began as a folded broadsheet handed out at a gallery opening, a quiet protest against images that scroll past too fast to be seen. We slow photography down: full plates, room to breathe, and writing that attends to how a picture was actually made.

We publish series rather than singles, because a body of work says what a lone frame cannot. We cover Process and Darkroom because the decisions — the developer, the dodge, the half-second — are where photographs are authored. Gear Notes are about seeing, not spec sheets.

Everything runs in black and white, by choice, because tone is where we want your attention. We credit the photographer prominently, describe the technique honestly, and let the plates hold the floor. Our interviews go to the question every photographer dodges: why this, and why now?

Aperture Null is independent and made by people who still love the smell of fixer. The newsletter sends one series worth sitting with each week, plus a single plate to carry into your day.

Editorial mission

To slow photography down — full plates, honest process, and the craft of the darkroom, with the image given room to be seen.
