Protein at Breakfast: What the Studies Say Minus the Shouting

Thirty grams became a commandment online. The evidence is friendlier and more flexible than the influencers are.

DV

Dr. Idris Vance

Jun 4, 2026·8 min read

The advice that finally worked was the advice I had been rolling my eyes at for years. Thirty grams became a commandment online. The evidence is friendlier and more flexible than the influencers are.

What follows is less a verdict than a working argument, assembled from notes, false starts and one or two opinions I have since revised. The headline is up top; everything below it has to earn the claim or quietly take it back.

Why the dull advice works

The advice that finally worked was the advice I had been rolling my eyes at for years: sleep first, move daily, eat food my grandmother would recognise. The boring fundamentals are boring precisely because they are settled, and settled because they are true.

Wellness culture sells complexity because complexity sells supplements. The honest protocol fits on an index card and costs almost nothing, which is exactly why it is so hard to market and so easy to dismiss in favour of the next miracle.

I am not interested in optimisation or biohacking my way to immortality. I am interested in the unglamorous floor of feeling decent most days, which turns out to be available to almost anyone willing to be consistent and unimpressed by trends.

What earned a permanent place

  • Sleep treated as non-negotiable infrastructure rather than the thing that gets cut first.
  • Movement that I actually enjoy, because the best exercise is the one I will still do in March.
  • Meals built from plants and patience, not powders and promises.

How to make it stick

Be patient with the boring middle. Beginnings are theatrical and endings are quotable, but the middle is where the real decisions live, unglamorous and undocumented. The block below is the part I would clip and keep.

Three things to carry out the door

  • Start smaller than feels respectable; momentum is the only currency that compounds.
  • Write down the thing you are afraid to be wrong about. Revisit it in a month.
  • Borrow ruthlessly, attribute generously, and keep the version that survives contact with reality.

None of this is medical advice, and a good clinician beats any article. But the fundamentals are forgiving and free: sleep, move, eat real food, repeat. The boring floor is higher than the exciting ceiling most products are selling.
