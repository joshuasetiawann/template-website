'A Catalogue of Small Departures' Turns Train Stations Into Elegy

Historian Ila Brandt walks ninety closed rural platforms and finds an entire social history in the waiting rooms.

EV

Eleanor Voss

Jun 4, 2026·9 min read

I finished the last page on a delayed train and sat with it past my stop. Historian Ila Brandt walks ninety closed rural platforms and finds an entire social history in the waiting rooms.

What follows is less a verdict than a working argument, assembled from notes, false starts and one or two opinions I have since revised. The headline is up top; everything below it has to earn the claim or quietly take it back.

What the book is doing

The prose does in a single sentence what lesser books need a chapter to attempt. This is a novel that lingers on interiority on purpose, that trusts the reader to sit in discomfort without being handed an exit, and it is the better for the patience it demands.

Plot is not the engine here; consciousness is. If you read for incident you will fidget. If you read for the particular pleasure of a mind rendered with almost unbearable precision, this will be among the best things you read this year.

It is not without flaws. A late turn strains credulity, and one character exists mainly to be argued with. But the writing earns enormous goodwill, and by the final pages the small concessions feel like a fair price for the rest.

What stayed with me after the last page

  • Sentences I read twice, not to understand them but to watch them work.
  • A central relationship rendered without sentiment and somehow all the more moving for it.
  • An ending that refuses to resolve and is exactly right to refuse.

Who it is for, and who it is not

Be patient with the boring middle. Beginnings are theatrical and endings are quotable, but the middle is where the real decisions live, unglamorous and undocumented. The block below is the part I would clip and keep.

The verdict

3.5/5

Read it for
The prose, which does in a sentence what lesser books need a chapter to attempt.
Skip it if
You want plot machinery over interiority; this one lingers on purpose.
Pairs with
A long train journey and a notebook you do not mind defacing.

Read it slowly, somewhere you will not be interrupted. This is not a book to be consumed between stops; it is one to be sat with, argued with, and quietly carried around for a while after the cover is closed.
