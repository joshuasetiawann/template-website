Our story

How this masthead came to be

The Marginalia Review takes its name from the pencil arguments in the gutters of well-read books. That is the spirit: criticism as a conversation with the text, generous but not soft, rigorous but never cold. We started because too many reviews had become either blurbs or takedowns, and the considered middle had gone quiet.

We review fiction, nonfiction, poetry, classics and books in translation, with critics who read the whole thing and quote it accurately. We believe a review's first duty is to describe the book the author actually wrote — not the one the critic wishes they had — and only then to judge it.

Translation gets a dedicated place here because the translator is a co-author, and we name them and weigh their work. Classics get rereadings rather than reverence. New voices get the same close attention as established ones, because the point is the page, not the reputation.

The Marginalia Review is independent and quietly opinionated. The Flyleaf newsletter sends one book worth your shelf a week, plus the sentence we couldn't stop thinking about.

Editorial mission

To practise criticism as conversation — generous, rigorous reviews that describe the book before judging it, and credit the translator as a co-author.

How we work

Three rules we don't bend

The standards behind everything we publish.

01

Read the whole thing

Every review is of the complete book, quoted accurately. We describe what the author actually wrote before we judge it.

02

Credit the translator

Translated work names its translator and weighs their craft. A translation is a co-authorship, not a transparency.

03

Conversation, not verdict

Criticism here is generous and rigorous at once — a pencil argument in the margin, not a blurb or a takedown.
