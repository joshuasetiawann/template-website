Our story

How this masthead came to be

The Wanderlog is a notebook that happens to travel. It started on a six-week train journey with no itinerary and one rule: write down the place before photographing it. The photographs faded; the field notes didn't. That felt like a lesson worth building a journal around.

We write slow travel because the interesting part of a place is rarely on the highlight reel. It is the ferry that runs whenever it feels like it, the grandmother who feeds you for refusing payment, the wrong turn that becomes the whole trip. We go the long way round on purpose.

Every dispatch is reported on the ground, in person, paying for our own coffee. We do not take comped trips that come with conditions, and we tell you what a place actually costs — in money, in effort, in the small frictions the brochures leave out. Practicalities get their own section because they matter.

The Wanderlog is for people who would rather understand one valley than tick off ten cities. The letter goes out when we have something worth the postage, which is to say, when we have actually been somewhere.

Editorial mission

To travel slowly and write it down honestly — the long way round, the real costs, and the places that don't make the highlight reel.
