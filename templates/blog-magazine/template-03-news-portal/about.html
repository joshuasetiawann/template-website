Our story

How this masthead came to be

The Daily Meridian was founded on a simple frustration: the news had gotten faster but not clearer. Headlines updated by the minute while the context that made them mean anything went missing. We set out to build a front page that respected both the urgency of the day and the patience of a reader.

Our newsroom is small, independent, and reader-funded — which means our only incentive is to be trusted twice: once when you read a story, and again when you come back. We separate reporting from opinion clearly, label analysis as analysis, and correct the record in public when we get something wrong.

We cover five desks — World, Politics, Economy, Climate and Culture — with reporters who specialise rather than generalise. A climate story is read by someone who knows the difference between a degree of warming and a degree of certainty. A markets story is checked against the filing, not the press release.

The morning Brief distils the day into the few things that genuinely changed, with links to the full reporting underneath. No outrage quotas, no engagement bait — just the desk, doing the work, on the record.

Editorial mission

To report the day accurately and in context, separate fact from opinion plainly, and earn the kind of trust that survives a correction.

How we work

Three rules we don't bend

The standards behind everything we publish.

01

Report, then publish

Stories run when they are confirmed by people and documents, not when they are first to the timeline. Speed never overrides sourcing.

02

Label everything

Reporting, analysis and opinion are marked clearly and kept apart, so you always know what you are reading and why.

03

Correct in public

When we are wrong, the correction is visible, dated, and explained. The record matters more than the ego.
