The Year I Stopped Optimizing My Mornings

I deleted the habit tracker, let the coffee take eleven minutes, and discovered that a slow start is not a moral failure.

JC

June Calloway

Jun 4, 2026·8 min read

I have been circling this one for weeks, the way you circle a cold pool. I deleted the habit tracker, let the coffee take eleven minutes, and discovered that a slow start is not a moral failure.

What follows is less a verdict than a working argument, assembled from notes, false starts and one or two opinions I have since revised. The headline is up top; everything below it has to earn the claim or quietly take it back.

What the notebook is actually for

I write longhand first because the keyboard is too eager to be read. A pencil has no audience and no autocomplete, and so it tells the truth a little more often. The notebook is where I find out what I think before I have decided to defend it.

People assume the point is to remember. It isn't. Almost nothing in these pages gets reread. The point is the noticing itself — the small daily act of deciding that a moment was worth the friction of a sentence.

If there is a craft here, it is the craft of paying attention without a plan to monetise it. The grocer alphabetising his apples does not become content. He becomes, briefly, the most important thing on the street.

What I keep in the back pages

  • A list of sentences overheard and stolen, attributed only to 'a man on the 4 train'.
  • The single best thing I ate each week, described in more detail than it deserved.
  • Apologies I owed and never sent, which is its own kind of filing system.

A practice, not a performance

Be patient with the boring middle. Beginnings are theatrical and endings are quotable, but the middle is where the real decisions live, unglamorous and undocumented. The block below is the part I would clip and keep.

Three things to carry out the door

  • Start smaller than feels respectable; momentum is the only currency that compounds.
  • Write down the thing you are afraid to be wrong about. Revisit it in a month.
  • Borrow ruthlessly, attribute generously, and keep the version that survives contact with reality.

None of this needs a reader to be worth the ink. If a line here sends you looking for your own pencil, the notebook has done the only thing I ask of it.

JC
