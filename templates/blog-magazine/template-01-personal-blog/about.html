Our story

How this masthead came to be

Inkwell & Ash began in 2019 as a shoebox of pocket notebooks and a refusal to perform. June Calloway had spent a decade writing copy that had to convert, and wanted, badly, to write something that only had to be true. The first essay went out to eleven friends. The second went out to nine, because two unsubscribed, which felt like exactly the right amount of honesty.

Everything here is written longhand first, at the same desk, beside the same ash tree. The keyboard is too eager to be read; a pencil has no audience and no autocomplete, so it tells the truth a little more often. By the time a piece reaches this page it has been argued with, crossed out, and quietly rebuilt at least twice.

There is no content calendar and no growth strategy. There is a kettle, a stack of index cards, and a standing rule that nothing gets published until it has survived a second reading on a second day. The result is slow on purpose: one essay or letter at a time, sent when it is ready rather than when the algorithm is hungry.

If the magazine has a thesis, it is that paying attention is its own reward and does not need to be monetised to count. The grocer who alphabetises his apples is not content; he is, briefly, the most important thing on the street. This is a place to write that down before deciding whether to defend it.

Editorial mission

To keep a notebook in public without letting the public turn it into a performance — slow essays, honest letters, and the small daily act of noticing.

The people behind the work

A small team that argues well and credits generously.

JC

June Calloway

Writer & sole proprietor

June has written every word here since 2019, typed on a 1974 Hermes when the neighbours are out. Essays on grief, groceries, slowness and the occasional triumphant soup.

M

Marlow

Reader-in-residence (cat)

Marlow reviews each draft by sitting on it. A piece is finished when he leaves the room. His editorial standards are opaque but consistent.

TG

The Tuesday Group

First readers

Four friends who get every essay a day early and are permitted, even encouraged, to write back the single word 'no'.

GC

A Guest Correspondent

Once a season, someone else's notebook gets a page here — a bakery owner, a night nurse, a stranger who returned a bicycle.

How we work

Three rules we don't bend

The standards behind everything we publish.

01

Longhand first

Every essay starts on paper, away from the cursor's blink, where sentences have to earn their place without a delete key.

02

A second day

Nothing publishes the day it is written. It rests overnight and is read again cold, which is where most of the lies get caught.

03

Sent when ready

No schedule, no roundups. One letter lands in your inbox on the Sunday it is actually finished, and not a Sunday before.

Want to get in touch?

Pitches, questions, corrections and collaborations all reach us at the desk. We read everything.
