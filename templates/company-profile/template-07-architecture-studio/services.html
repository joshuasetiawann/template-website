The index, unabridged

Six disciplines. Three buildings, accounted for.

Every discipline below is set out the way we draw: scope first, fee plainly stated, method in three movements. After the disciplines come three built works recorded with programme, budget and completion — the only review that matters is the building standing.

Scope & fee

What each discipline includes

Same six rows as the homepage index, in the same order. Cost plans begin in week two of any commission.

01

Civic & Cultural Buildings

Fee by stage — typically 6–9% of build

Libraries, chapels and community halls — public rooms designed for fifty years of changing use. We carry these commissions from competition or feasibility through tender to the five-year revisit, with the town, not just the client, treated as the end user.

What's included

  • Briefing sessions with every user group, staff to schoolchildren
  • Daylight and acoustic studies before the first plan
  • Planning and heritage submissions, argued in person
  • Full detail set to joinery level; one drawing, one price
  • One-year and five-year revisits, written up honestly

Method

  1. iWalk the site at dawn; brief everyone who will use the room.
  2. iiOne costed scheme, defended in a single long meeting.
  3. iiiDetail, tender, build — weekly site presence throughout.
02

Private Houses

Fee from 9% of build cost

Site-specific homes built around light studies, with detail drawings down to the door pull. We take four houses a year; each begins with a fortnight of listening and ends with the architects helping carry furniture in.

What's included

  • Sun studies across four seasons, drawn before plans
  • Cost plan from week two, updated at every stage
  • Detail drawings to the door pull and stair nosing
  • Contractor selection and weekly site inspection

Method

  1. iLive with the site — dawn and dusk walks, soil and sky.
  2. iiHand-drawn concept, costed, argued once, agreed.
  3. iiiBuild with weekly presence; revisit at year one and five.
03

Workplace & Studios

From €52,000 design fee

Offices that borrow the logic of workshops: daylight benches, quiet cores, honest materials. We design for the people who stay until seven, not for the lobby photograph — though the photograph tends to follow.

What's included

  • Workplace study: how the team actually moves and meets
  • Daylight benching plan and acoustic zoning
  • Fixed joinery and furniture drawn by the same hand
  • Phasing plan so the business never fully stops

Method

  1. iShadow the team for a week; count the real meetings.
  2. iiTest-fit options costed against the lease, not the render.
  3. iiiDeliver in phases; measure noise and light after move-in.
04

Adaptive Reuse

Fee from 10% of works

Barns, depots and mills given second lives without erasing their first ones. The existing structure is surveyed as carefully as a client is interviewed; what can stay, stays, and what must change is confessed in the detailing.

What's included

  • Measured survey and structural appraisal with our engineers
  • Retention schedule: what stays, what goes, and why
  • Heritage consents and conservation officer liaison
  • New work detailed as honestly new — no imitation surfaces
  • Embodied-carbon ledger comparing reuse against rebuild

Method

  1. iListen to the structure — survey, open up, test.
  2. iiDecide what the building wants to keep doing.
  3. iiiAdd the new plainly; let the seams show on purpose.
05

Interiors & Furniture

From €18,000

Joinery, fixed furniture and material palettes drawn by the same hand as the building. Pieces are designed for the room they stand in and built by workshops we have used for a decade — nothing is specified from a catalogue first.

What's included

  • Material palette with full-size samples, not swatches
  • Fixed joinery drawn at 1:5 and 1:1 where it matters
  • Workshop tendering and prototype review
  • Lighting plan measured in evenings, not lux tables alone

Method

  1. iInventory the life of the room — hands, habits, hours.
  2. iiDraw the pieces with the building's own details.
  3. iiiPrototype, adjust, install with the maker present.
06

Feasibility & Massing Studies

Fixed €12,500 — two weeks

Two-week studies that tell you what a site wants to be — before you buy it. Half our studies end with a recommendation not to proceed; clients tell us that page paid for itself several times over.

What's included

  • Site walk and constraints map: light, noise, neighbours, ground
  • Three massing options with honest area schedules
  • Planning-risk reading from precedent decisions
  • Cost band per option and a plain go / no-go page

Method

  1. iWeek one: walk, measure, read the planning record.
  2. iiWeek two: three massings, costed and ranked.
  3. iiiOne meeting: we recommend, you decide.
Built studies

Three works, recorded plainly

Programme, budget, completion. The photographs are elsewhere; these are the facts a building should be able to state for itself.

Fjellhus — house under a ridge

Private house · Norway

ProgrammeA four-bedroom family house on a steep coastal plot, set beneath the ridge rather than upon it. The roof follows the hill's own line; every habitable room was placed by its hours of sun before the plan was drawn.

OutcomeCompleted in 2024 after sixteen months on site, within the cost plan agreed at concept. The clients report the awards matter less than a plan the family actually lives in — which is the review we keep.

212m² under one ridge-line roof
€1.85MBuild cost — held to the week-two plan
2024Completed; year-one revisit done

Aker Reading Rooms

Civic library · Oslo

ProgrammeA district library replacing a 1970s branch: reading rooms, a children's floor and a hall the neighbourhood can book. The brief asked for quiet; the daylight studies decided nearly everything else.

OutcomeHanded over in 2023 on a municipal budget, with two-thirds of opening hours needing no artificial light in the main rooms. Visits in the first year ran far past the projection — the building is now open four evenings a week.

2,400m² of public rooms
NOK 96MConstruction budget, met
68%Daylight autonomy, measured year one

Tannery 4 — studio conversion

Adaptive reuse · Rotterdam

ProgrammeA redundant leather works turned into studios for makers and small practices. The brief: keep the building's first life legible while making the second one warm, dry and affordable to rent.

OutcomeCompleted 2022. More than three-quarters of the original structure was retained, the embodied-carbon ledger showed reuse beating rebuild decisively, and the waiting list for studios opened before the paint dried.

78%Original structure retained
3,150m² of studios and shared workshops
2022Completed — fully let since opening
