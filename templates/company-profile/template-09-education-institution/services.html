Pathways in depth

The curriculum, shown working.

A prospectus tells you what a school intends; this page shows what Alder Gate actually teaches, stage by stage, with fees stated plainly and bursaries at every entry point. Beneath the pathways sit three accounts of college life from the past year — told in full, with the numbers.

Stage by stage

Six pathways, one quadrangle

The same six programmes from the homepage tabs, set out the way the academic deputy explains them on an open morning.

Years 7–8 Foundations

The broadest two years a pupil will ever study: two languages from day one, laboratory science taught in laboratories, and weekly debate so every eleven-year-old learns to stand and be heard. Classes are capped at eighteen and average fourteen.

What the stage includes

  • Two modern languages plus Latin taster term
  • Three sciences taught to lab standard from Year 7
  • Weekly debate and public reading in house groups
  • A tutor who meets your child twice a week
  • Written reports each half term — composed, not generated

How the stage unfolds

  1. 1Settle. A September of diagnostics and tutor conversations, not tests.
  2. 2Broaden. Every subject, every term — no narrowing before Year 9.
  3. 3Review. A written profile guides GCSE choices at the end of Year 8.

Fees from £4,950 per term · bursaries fund 1 in 6 places

Outdoor Curriculum

Fieldwork, navigation and a three-day expedition every term, because some lessons cannot be taught at a desk. Pupils plan routes, carry their own kit and come home having led something — rain is considered part of the syllabus.

What the stage includes

  • A three-day expedition every term, all years
  • Map, compass and route-planning taught formally
  • Geography and biology fieldwork with real datasets
  • Cross-country, fell-walking and expedition fitness
  • Qualified expedition staff and a 1:6 supervision ratio

How the stage unfolds

  1. 1Train. Navigation and camp-craft on campus before any hill.
  2. 2Expedition. Pupils lead legs of the route in rotation.
  3. 3Debrief. Field notebooks marked; the next trip planned by pupils.

Included in fees · residential expeditions from £180

GCSE Programme

Ten subjects with setted mathematics and triple science as standard, taught by subject specialists who still publish and perform. The tutoring system catches gaps in week three, not at the mock examinations.

What the stage includes

  • Ten GCSEs with triple science as standard
  • Setted mathematics reviewed every half term
  • Continued languages — seven offered to examination
  • Study-skills course taught, not assumed
  • Twice-weekly tutorials tracking each subject honestly

How the stage unfolds

  1. 1Choose. Options built around the Year 8 profile, with parents in the room.
  2. 2Deepen. Two years of specialist teaching and early gap-catching.
  3. 3Examine. Mocks marked to board standard; results explained, not posted.

Fees from £5,250 per term · means-tested support available

Rhetoric & Performance

Every pupil speaks, stages or exhibits publicly once a year — a rule as old as the college. Debate, drama, music and the sculpture court all count; the requirement is the standing up, not the polish.

What the stage includes

  • A guaranteed public moment for every pupil, every year
  • Debating society coached by practising barristers
  • A full opera or play staged each spring
  • Exhibition space in the sculpture court each summer
  • Voice and presence coaching in small groups

How the stage unfolds

  1. 1Pick a stage. Speech, performance or exhibition — pupil’s choice.
  2. 2Rehearse. Weekly coaching toward a real audience date.
  3. 3Perform. Family invited; the programme keeps every name.

Included in fees at every stage — no performance levy

A-Level Pathways

Twenty-six subjects with university-style tutorials in groups of six, so a Sixth Former is questioned, stretched and known — not lectured at. 92% of the most recent cohort took up their first-choice university place.

What the stage includes

  • Twenty-six A-Level subjects, combinations kept open
  • Tutorials in groups of six, essays defended aloud
  • University and apprenticeship counsel from Year 12 week one
  • Interview and admissions-test preparation in house
  • Sixth Form common room and supervised study till 18:00

How the stage unfolds

  1. 1Select. Three or four subjects chosen against a destinations plan.
  2. 2Tutor. Weekly six-person tutorials alongside taught lessons.
  3. 3Launch. Applications, interviews and results-day support to the end.

Sixth Form from £5,600 per term · scholarships at entry

Extended Research

A supervised year-long thesis on a question the pupil chooses, defended before visiting academics in the summer term. Recent titles ranged from canal-water microbiology to the economics of lower-league football.

What the stage includes

  • A named research supervisor for the full year
  • Library, laboratory and archive access out of hours
  • Research methods seminars each half term
  • Observatory and field-station time for science theses
  • A public defence before visiting academics

How the stage unfolds

  1. 1Question. A proposal argued for and approved by October.
  2. 2Research. Two terms of supervised reading, fieldwork and drafting.
  3. 3Defend. The thesis presented and questioned in the summer term.

Included for Sixth Form — no project levy

Academic · Debating Society

National Debating Finals — champions

The challenge. A finals draw against three larger schools with professional coaching budgets, and a motion — on water rights — announced only one hour before the first round.

The result. The senior four argued both sides of every motion unbeaten across the weekend, the first national title since 2019 — built on nothing more exotic than weekly debate from Year 7.

Arts · Spring production

Spring Opera: The Magic Flute

The challenge. Mozart, in the original German, with an orchestra pit full of teenagers and a Queen of the Night aria that has ended braver productions than ours.

The result. Four sold-out nights in the college hall. Every voice on stage and every instrument in the pit belonged to a pupil; the German was coached by the languages department at lunchtimes.

Sport · First XI Hockey

First XI Hockey County Cup

The challenge. A county final against the holders, reached by a squad drawn from a college of 460 — half the playing pool of every other school in the bracket.

The result. The cup came back to the quadrangle after a 2–1 final, ending an unbeaten season built on expedition fitness and a goalkeeper who also plays the trombone.

Asked at every open morning

Admissions questions, answered

A taster day in lessons, a short written paper in English and mathematics, and a conversation with a tutor. We are assessing curiosity and habits of work, not coaching — heavily tutored applications are easy to spot and rarely helped by it.

Means-tested bursaries fund between 30% and 100% of fees and currently support one place in six. They exist at all three entry points; the registrar will tell you within a week whether an application is realistic.

Yes — roughly twenty external pupils join Year 12 each September. Offers are based on GCSE predictions and a subject interview, and every external entrant is paired with a current Sixth Former for the first term.

Lunch, books, performances and the research programme are inside the fee. Residential expeditions (from £180), music lessons and examination entries are the listed extras — itemised in writing before you accept a place.
