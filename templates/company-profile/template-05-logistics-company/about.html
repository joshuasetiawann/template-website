About MeridianHaul

Freight, warehousing and last-mile — run by people who answer the phone.

MeridianHaul Logistics moves freight across the country and the final mile to the door, with the visibility of a tech platform and the accountability of a dispatcher who knows your name.

Our story

Built in a yard, not a boardroom

MeridianHaul Logistics started in 2005 with six trucks, a leased yard outside Kansas City, and a founder who had spent fifteen years as a dispatcher watching freight brokers overpromise and underdeliver. The pitch was simple and unglamorous: pick up when we say, deliver when we say, and answer the phone when something goes wrong. Two decades later that is still the entire strategy.

We grew from regional trucking into a full logistics operation — long-haul freight, cross-dock warehousing, and a last-mile delivery network — because our customers kept asking us to take on the next link in their chain. We never bolted on a service we could not run properly. Each capability earned its place by working, then scaled.

Logistics has discovered technology, and so have we, but we use it to keep promises rather than to replace people. Every shipment is tracked in real time and visible to the customer, every exception triggers a call from a named coordinator, and our warehouse runs on barcodes and discipline rather than hope. Visibility without accountability is just a prettier way to watch a delivery be late.

Our drivers and warehouse crews are mostly long-tenured and on our own payroll, not a rotating cast of subcontractors. That is why our on-time rate sits where it does and why customers trust us with freight that cannot afford to sit. A reliable carrier is boring, and boring is exactly what a supply chain wants.

Twenty years on, MeridianHaul runs a national lane network and three distribution centres, and the founder still spends part of every week in dispatch. The trucks are newer and the software is better, but the promise has not moved an inch.
