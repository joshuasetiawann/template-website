Our Story

Started in a farm shed, sauced ever since

Rootline began in a shed. Chef Imani Okafor ran a series of harvest dinners at Marigold Acres, cooking whatever the fields gave up that week on long tables under work-lights, with the soil practically still on the plates.

The dinners sold out faster than the farm could plant, so Imani took the idea indoors and gave it a roof on Marigold Lane. The connection to the farm never loosened — much of the menu still arrives the morning it’s picked, in crates, not boxes.

Everything here is plant-based, but the kitchen treats vegetables like the main event they are, not a consolation prize. We roast hard, ferment patiently and season fearlessly, because a carrot cooked properly does not need to pretend to be anything else.

We name our growers on the menu because they did the hard part. The chefs’ job is to get out of the way of a perfect tomato, and to coax the most out of the humbler stuff — the roots, the leaves, the things that don’t usually get top billing.

The room is leafy and unfussy, the brunch is generous, and the patio fills up the second the sun comes out. Come for the bowls, stay because nobody’s rushing you out of your seat.

Sourcing

Plants don’t need apologies, they need heat and salt

Farm-direct

Much of the menu comes from Marigold Acres and a handful of nearby growers, picked the morning it’s served and credited by name.

Roasted, not hidden

We cook vegetables hard — roasted, charred, fermented — so they taste of themselves. No imitation, no apology, just technique.

Whole-plant, low-waste

Stalks, tops and trimmings become stocks, pickles and sauces. A whole plant goes a long way when nothing is thrown out.
