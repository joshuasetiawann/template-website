Our Story

A marathon, a juicer, a tiny shop

Pulp & Glow began with a grudge against sports drinks. Founder Mae Santos was training for a marathon, tired of neon bottles full of things she couldn’t pronounce, and one day she dragged a hand-me-down cold press home and started juicing her own.

Friends asked for bottles, then strangers at the trailhead asked for bottles, and before long the tiny shop on Grove Parade had a queue of runners out front. The whole idea was simple: if the juice needs a label to convince you, the fruit wasn’t ripe enough.

Everything is pressed and blended to order from whole fruit and veg — no concentrates, no powders pretending to be food, no added sugar hiding in the back. The menu changes with what’s ripe, so the board leans tropical in summer and citrus-and-root in winter.

Mae still does most of the early pressing herself, and the shop runs on small, fresh batches because cold-pressed juice doesn’t keep and shouldn’t. What’s pressed in the morning is sold that day; whatever pulp is left goes to compost, not the bin.

It sits right by the runners’ trailhead, which is exactly the point — a bright, clean, no-nonsense stop for something that actually tastes like the fruit it came from. Bring your bottle back and the next one’s a dollar off.
