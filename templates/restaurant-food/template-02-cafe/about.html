Our Story

Two roasters, one corner shop

Siblings June and Marcus Harrow roasted their first sacks of Yirgacheffe on a repurposed popcorn drum in 2015, in a garage that smelled permanently of caramel. Friends started leaving money in a jam jar; the jam jar became a waitlist.

Two years and one very forgiving landlord later, the corner shop on Alder Street became home. They kept the garage habits — roasting in tiny batches, tasting everything, throwing out anything that drifted past its best — and added a marble counter and a very loud espresso machine.

Everything is still small-batch. Coffee is roasted twice a week so nothing sits, buns are knotted before sunrise, and the brew bar runs on a simple rule: the kettle never boils twice for the same cup.

June handles the roast and the relationships with farms; Marcus runs the bar and the pastry case. Between them they have trained every barista who has ever pulled a shot here, usually over a very long, very opinionated tasting.

We think of the café as the last, gentle step of farming someone else started months earlier and a continent away. Our job is simply not to ruin it — and, on a good morning, to make it taste like the farmer hoped it would.

Sourcing

Coffee is just farming, finished gently

Single-origin, traceable

We buy from farms and co-ops we can name, at prices we are not embarrassed to print. Every bag lists the origin, the altitude and the harvest.

Roasted twice a week

Beans are roasted in small drums and rested for days, not weeks. If a batch drifts past its window, it goes home with the staff, not into your cup.

Slow bar, fast smiles

The brew bar is unhurried on purpose. We would rather make one cup properly than three in a rush — and we will happily talk you through the difference.
