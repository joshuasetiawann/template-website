Instructors

Chefs who'd rather teach than plate

Copper Spoon Atelier exists because Rosa Lindqvist kept getting distracted at the pass. A career chef, she found herself forever turning to explain a technique to whoever was beside her — why the pan was that hot, why the dough needed to rest — instead of just plating the dish and moving on.

Eventually she admitted she liked the explaining more than the expediting, hung up the whites, and opened a studio loft full of induction hobs and good light. The Atelier is built around one belief: a recipe isn’t learned until your hands stop reading it.

Classes are small and hands-on. Everyone gets a station, a copper-bottom pan and an apron, and nobody watches from the back — you cook the whole thing yourself, start to finish, with an instructor at your shoulder rather than on a stage.

The instructors are all working or former chefs who, like Rosa, would rather teach than perform. They cover the why as much as the how, so you leave understanding heat, salt and timing, not just one dish you can never quite repeat at home.

It’s a generous, slightly messy, very welcoming place. Knives and aprons are provided, the recipe cards are yours to keep, and the rule is simple: make the mistakes here, in good company, so the dinner at home goes right.
