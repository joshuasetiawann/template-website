Our Oven

The oven came first, the shop came after

Most restaurants pick a space and squeeze in a kitchen. Gia and Paolo Russo did it backwards: they bought a wood-fired oven in Naples, shipped three tonnes of brick and dome across an ocean, and then went looking for a building that could hold it.

Carmine Avenue won because the floor could take the weight and the chimney could be cut where the oven wanted it. The dining room was designed afterwards, around the heat, which is why every seat has a view of the flames.

The dough is the other obsession. It ferments slowly — a calendar, not a clock — so the cornicione puffs and chars the way it should and sits lightly afterwards. Paolo mixes it; Gia runs the floor; the oven, frankly, runs everyone.

We cook at the temperature Naples taught us, which means a pizza is in and out in ninety seconds and the leopard-spotting on the crust is a feature, not a flaw. The first pie out each night still goes to whoever lit the fire that morning.

It is a family shop in the old sense — loud, generous, occasionally chaotic. We import the things that matter, make the rest ourselves, and treat the oven like the senior partner it is.

How we cook

Good dough doesn’t need a clock, it needs a calendar

One wood-fired oven

Three tonnes of Neapolitan brick, fired with seasoned hardwood. Ninety seconds at the right heat is the whole secret.

Slow-fermented dough

Mixed simply and left to prove for a day or more, so the crust is light, blistered and easy to eat.

Imported where it counts

San Marzano tomatoes, fior di latte and good olive oil come from people we trust; the basil comes from the windowsill.
