The Fleet

Couriers who treat food like cargo that matters

Platerunner started with a single e-bike and a stubborn thesis: delivery shouldn’t mean soggy. Founder Dani Okoye had spent enough years as a courier to know that most cold, sad takeaway is a logistics failure, not a kitchen one.

So she built the company around the ride, not just the app. Insulated bags, short and smart routes, and a simple promise to every restaurant on the platform: we don’t deliver food, we deliver the kitchen’s reputation. Treat the cargo like it matters and it arrives like it matters.

The fleet is local and rider-first. Couriers know their zones, get paid fairly, and aren’t pushed to take routes that would let an order go cold. We cover nine zones across the city, and we’d rather grow slowly than stretch the map until quality slips.

Behind the scenes, dispatch watches the kitchens and the weather, batches sensibly, and keeps the average order moving in well under the time it takes to go cold. The trending dishes on the app are the ones that travel best — because some food is built for the journey.

We partner with kitchens we actually rate, list honestly, and answer the chat ourselves. It’s delivery run by people who care whether the lid stayed on — which, it turns out, is most of the battle.
