Our Story

A restaurant written by fire and the market

Twelve courses, twenty-two seats and one open hearth — here is how Lumière & Ash came to cook the way it does.

EElena MarchettiChef & Patron
The Chef

Cooking that begins at the fire

Lumière & Ash opened in 2014 with a single, stubborn idea: every course should pass through flame before it reaches the table. No induction rings, no sous-vide baths hidden in the back — just a hearth, hardwood, and the patience to let heat do the talking.

Chef-patron Elena Marchetti built the room around that fire after a decade in the kitchens of Lyon and Copenhagen. She came home wanting less — fewer seats, fewer covers, fewer tricks — and more time with each plate. The result is a counter and a handful of tables that turn just twice a night.

The menu is written every Tuesday, after the market, never before. What the growers and the coast hand over that week decides the twelve courses; the kitchen simply arranges them into an arc that moves from raw and bright through smoke and ember into sweetness and ash.

Service is deliberately quiet. Twelve cooks work the pass in near silence, plates are carried rather than announced, and the loudest sound in the room is usually the fire itself. We would rather you forget we are working at all.

Nothing here is rushed, and nothing is permanent — the card you read tonight will be gone by next week. That impermanence is the point: a dinner at Lumière & Ash is a portrait of one particular fortnight, cooked once and never repeated exactly.

Philosophy

Fire first, then restraint

Everything touches flame

Hearth-cooking is not a garnish here — it is the method. Hardwood embers, live coals and patience replace most of the gadgets a modern kitchen leans on.

Written by the market

We buy what is best that week and build the menu around it, so sourcing comes before recipes. Growers and day-boats are credited on the pass, not buried.

Restraint as luxury

The most expensive ingredient we use is the courage to leave things off the plate. Three elements, perfectly timed, beat thirty competing for attention.
