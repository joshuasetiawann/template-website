Our Story

One plancha, four wheels, zero landlords

Citrus Comet began at a government auction, where line cook Manny Reyes bid on a retired postal van mostly as a joke and won it for less than a month’s rent. He gutted it, dropped in a plancha, painted it the colour of a ripe lemon, and never looked back.

The pitch was simple: a tiny menu, a screaming-hot plancha, and no landlord to answer to. The truck goes where the people are — office blocks at lunch, breweries at night, markets on the weekend — and the location board changes every week.

Manny cooks, drives and owns the whole operation, with a rotating crew of two on the busy stops. The salsas are made at the commissary each morning, the tortillas are pressed to order, and the limes — there are always a lot of limes — get squeezed onto everything.

Small menu, hot plancha, full send. That’s the entire business plan, and it works because nothing sits: tacos are built in front of you, fast and bright, and the line moves because the menu is short on purpose.

Find the truck on the weekly board, follow it on socials, or book it to roll up to your own party. Wherever it parks, the yellow van and the smell of charred onions tend to draw a crowd within minutes.
