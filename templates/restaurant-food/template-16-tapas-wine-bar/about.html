Our Story

A honeymoon that never quite ended

Cala Roja means red cove, and it’s a real one — a small, terracotta-coloured inlet on the Spanish coast where Marisol Vega and Ben Carter spent the last day of their honeymoon and quietly decided not to fly home on schedule.

They stayed for a season, eating their way through tiny tapas bars where nobody hurried and everybody shared. When they finally came back, they brought the whole rhythm with them: small plates, good sherry, long evenings, and the idea that tapas is a pace, not a portion.

The bar is built for grazing. Plates come out when they’re ready, not in courses; the menu leans on Spanish classics done properly — jamón, gildas, tortilla, plancha-blistered padrón — and the wine list runs deep on rioja, cava and sherry.

Sherry hour is the heart of the week. From five to seven, the fino and manzanilla flow, the bar fills with after-work regulars, and the little red tiles on the back wall catch the low light exactly the way the cove did.

Marisol runs the floor and the wine; Ben runs the kitchen and the plancha. Between them they’ve recreated a corner of that coast inland — unhurried, generous, and best enjoyed across a couple of hours and a lot of small plates.
