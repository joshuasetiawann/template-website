Our House

The dry-age room runs the show

Behind the host stand, a glass-walled room holds racks of beef sleeping at two degrees.

Emberline buys whole animals from six ranches and breaks them down in-house. The dry-age room — salt walls, slow fans, forty-five days minimum — decides the menu more than any chef does.

Pitmaster-turned-grillardin Marcus Bell runs the oak broiler at nine hundred degrees. Every steak rests as long as it seared. House rule: the kitchen will not cook past medium without a signed waiver. (We keep a pen. Nobody has asked twice.)
