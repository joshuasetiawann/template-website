Our Story

Three decades on the same pier

Saltline & Tide stands on a working pier, in the timber building where, for a century, the day’s catch was weighed and iced. When the old cannery closed, founder Rosa Delmar took the lease before the gulls had even noticed.

She brought in chef Tomás Rivera, and together they made a simple promise: the menu is written by the weather, and the kitchen just holds the chalk. What the day-boats land that morning is what you’ll eat that night — and if the sea keeps it ashore, so do we.

The raw bar is the heart of the room. Oysters are shucked to order, the catch board is rubbed out and rewritten daily, and the dishes are built to taste of salt, smoke and cold water rather than of the kitchen.

We know our boats by name and our oysters by bay. Provenance isn’t a marketing line here; it’s how you tell a good day’s fishing from a bad one. The closer the dock, the shorter the journey, the better the plate.

Three decades in, the pier still sets the pace. Service runs on the tide and the weather, the windows fog up when it’s cold, and the best seat in the house is the one closest to the water.
