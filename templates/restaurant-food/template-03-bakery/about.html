Our Story

A bakehouse with one oven and big feelings

Butterfold began with forty croissants, a folding table and a hand-painted sign at the Saturday market. They sold out by nine, every week, until it became obvious the stall needed a proper roof and a much bigger oven.

Head baker and owner Maren Ode found both on Quince Street — a narrow shopfront with a deck oven that has, ever since, set the pace of the whole operation. One oven means we bake in waves; the schedule on the wall is less a menu than a timetable.

Everything is laminated by hand and proofed slowly. The croissant dough is folded over two days, the sourdough is started the night before, and nothing is rushed to hit a number. If the butter isn’t cold, we genuinely start over.

Mornings begin in the dark. Maren and the team shape, score and load before the sun is up, so the first tray of pastry hits the case as the door unlocks. By noon, whatever is left moves to the day-old shelf at half price — we would rather sell it cheap than throw it away.

The bakehouse is small on purpose. We have resisted every suggestion to add a second oven, because the limit is the discipline: it keeps the range honest, the batches small and the crust exactly where we like it.

How we bake

Cold butter, slow time, one oven

Stone-milled flour

We bake with regional, stone-milled flours and let the grain show. The sourdough is naturally leavened with a starter older than the shop.

Two-day lamination

Croissant dough is folded and rested over two days, never forced. Slow proofing is why the layers shatter the way they do.

Nothing wasted

Whatever the case doesn’t sell becomes the half-price day-old shelf, croutons or bread pudding. The compost bucket is a last resort.
