Our Story

A physics teacher and a 1947 churn

Moonmilk started with an inheritance and an obsession. When Dee Okada was handed her grandfather’s 1947 salt-and-ice churn, she meant to keep it on a shelf. Three weeks later she was making five gallons of brown-butter ice cream a night.

Dee taught high-school physics, which turns out to be excellent training for ice cream — it’s all about ice crystals, fat, air and temperature. She kept the batches tiny so she could control every variable, and the scoop shop on Pippin Square grew out of that habit.

Everything is still small-batch and made on site. Custards are cooked low and slow, churned in little spins, and the flavours rotate with whatever’s good — orchard fruit in autumn, citrus in winter, herbs and berries when summer hits.

We churn five gallons because that’s honest; five hundred would be marketing. Small batches mean the texture stays dense and clean, nothing sits in the freezer long enough to get icy, and the menu can change on a whim.

The shop is all pastel and chrome, the cone window stays open a little past close, and there is always a line on a hot evening. We are completely fine with that — good ice cream is worth a short wait.

How we make it

Five gallons is honest, five hundred is marketing

Small-batch, on site

Every flavour is churned in little spins from a slow-cooked custard. Small batches keep the texture dense and the freezer turning over.

Rotating, seasonal

Flavours follow the season — orchard fruit, winter citrus, summer herbs and berries — so the board is never quite the same twice.

Controlled, not cut

No stabiliser shortcuts and no giant tanks. Just fat, air, ice and temperature, handled with a physics teacher’s patience.
