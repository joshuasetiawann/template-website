Our Story

A griddle older than the owners

The cast-iron flat-top came out of a shuttered diner — fifty years seasoned, two thousand pounds, non-negotiable.

Cousins Ray and Tonya Hatch towed the griddle across two state lines before they had a building to put it in. The shop was designed around it: counter up front, griddle in the window, smoke alarm hard-wired to be patient.

Beef is ground fresh every morning from chuck and brisket, smashed thin so the edges go lacy and the middle stays loose. It's a four-napkin operation and the neighbourhood likes it that way.
