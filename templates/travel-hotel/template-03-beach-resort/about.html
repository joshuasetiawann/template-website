Our story

Barefoot on the lagoon's edge

Coralline Bay began with a single jetty and a promise: build nothing that the reef would not forgive. Every villa stands on stilts driven by hand between the coral heads, every walkway follows the channels the fish already use, and the house reef you snorkel today is the reason the resort exists at all.

We are barefoot by design. There are no road shoes here, no lobby marble, no formality to perform. Sand runs up to the deck of each villa; the bar keeps your size in fins rather than slippers; and the dress code, such as it is, asks only that you rinse the salt off before dinner.

The kitchens cook what the boats bring in. Three barefoot kitchens around the bay change their menus with the catch and the tide, and the reef rangers will show you where your dinner was swimming that morning. Luxury, on this island, tastes of lime, char and the sea.

Above all we are custodians of a living reef. A marine biologist lives on site, the coral nursery is open to guests, and a share of every booking funds the rangers who keep the lagoon healthy for the turtles that nest on our beach each season.

Sustainability

A resort built around a reef

The reef came first and still does. Solar arrays carry most of the resort's power, water is desalinated and recycled on the island, and our coral nursery has replanted thousands of fragments along the house reef. A resident marine biologist guides the programme, and guests are welcome to take part.
