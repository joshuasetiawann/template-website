Our story

Out of the door and onto the snow

Glacier Hollow grew up around a single chairlift and a hut that sold soup. The mountain came first, and the resort was built to serve it rather than the other way round — which is why, decades later, you can still click in at your own door and ski to the lift without walking a step on tarmac.

Ski-in, ski-out is not a slogan here; it is the whole layout. The lodges line the home run, the boot rooms open straight onto the snow, and the last trail of the day delivers you to the terrace with your drink already on its way. First tracks and last orders are equally easy to reach.

The terrain is the reason to come. From the glacier at the top to the tree runs at the bottom, the mountain holds something for a first week on skis and for a season-pass local chasing the powder. Snowmaking covers the early weeks, the grooming is relentless, and the high bowls keep the snow long after the valley has thawed.

Off the snow, we keep it warm and unfussy. Wood-fired lodges, a spa to soak the aches out, long tables for tired legs and good food, and a ski school that has been teaching nervous beginners to love the mountain for as long as the lifts have turned.

Sustainability

Snow worth protecting

A ski resort lives or dies by the climate, so ours takes it seriously. Lifts run on renewable power, snowmaking is timed for efficiency and fed by a recycling reservoir, and we are restoring alpine meadow on the lower slopes for the green seasons. Protecting the mountain is, quite literally, protecting the business.
