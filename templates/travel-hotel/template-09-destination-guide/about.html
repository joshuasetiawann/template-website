Our story

Lisbon, unfolded by people who live here

Lisbon Unfolded is written by people who live in the city, not by an algorithm or an advertiser. It started as a folder of notes we kept passing to visiting friends — the tram that beats the queue, the miradouro without the crowd, the tasca where the bill is still written on the paper tablecloth.

We take no money to be listed. Nothing here is sponsored, ranked by who paid, or padded to fill a page. If a place is in the guide it is because one of us eats there, drinks there or sends their own family there — and if it slips, it comes out, no matter how famous it has become.

The guide is built around how the city actually works. We organise it by neighbourhood and by hour, because Lisbon rewards the patient: the same street is a different place at nine in the morning and nine at night, and the steep shortcut is only worth it if you know the view at the top.

Above all it is honest. We will tell you which famous pastel de nata is worth the line and which is not, which tram is a joy and which is a pickpocket's office, and when to simply put the map away and get gloriously, productively lost in the Alfama.

Heritage

A city worth treading lightly in

Lisbon is living with the strain of its own popularity, and a good guide should help, not pile on. We steer visitors beyond the few overrun streets, favour small independent places over chains, and remind readers that the miradouros are someone's neighbourhood. Travel kindly and the city stays worth visiting.
