Our story

Small groups, wild places, real depth

Greenline Expeditions has been running small-group nature journeys since 1994, back when ‘ecotourism’ was a word almost nobody used. The founders were field biologists who were tired of watching wildlife from the back of a crowded bus, and the company has never lost that bias toward small, slow and close.

We cap every departure at twelve travellers. It is the difference between a tour and a journey: small enough to fit one vehicle, to eat at one table, to fall silent together when something extraordinary walks out of the trees. Twelve curious people and a guide who knows the ground is, we think, the right size for the wild.

Our guides live where they lead. They are local naturalists first and hosts second — the kind of people who can name the bird before you have raised your binoculars and tell you which family runs the lodge you are sleeping in tonight. Their knowledge, and their relationships, are the whole trip.

We travel in a way the places can carry. We use locally owned lodges, keep group sizes low, offset what we cannot avoid and put a share of every booking into the conservancies we visit. Thirty years in, we are convinced the best journeys leave the wild a little better defended than they found it.

Sustainability

Conservation is the whole point

We do not bolt sustainability on at the end; it is why the company exists. Small groups, locally owned lodges, offset flights and a conservation levy on every booking mean each journey actively funds the habitats it visits. Thirty years of returning to the same places keeps us honest about our impact.
