Our story

Canvas, woodsmoke and a proper bed

Wildfern began as a hay meadow and a stubborn idea: that you could sleep close to the wild without giving up a good night's rest. So we pitched canvas instead of pouring concrete, raised every bed off the ground, lit a wood stove in each tent, and left the meadow to do what meadows do.

Everything here is the outdoors with the discomfort taken out. The tents are warm, the beds are deep, the showers are hot, and the hot tubs are fired by wood you split yourself if you fancy it. You wake to birdsong and dew on the canvas, not to a car park and a corridor.

We tread lightly on purpose. The pitches move on a rotation so the meadow recovers, the lighting is low enough to keep the night sky dark, and the only soundtrack is the stream, the owls and the occasional indignant pheasant. Nature is the amenity; we just added a kettle.

It is the kind of place that turns phones off by themselves. Families light fires and toast questionable marshmallows; couples disappear into a hot tub under the stars; and everyone, sooner or later, ends up around the fire pit swapping the day's small adventures.

Sustainability

Light on the land by design

Wildfern is built to leave almost no trace. Pitches rotate to let the meadow recover, water is heated by wood and sun, composting loos keep the ground clean, and our lighting is kept low to protect the dark sky and the wildlife that depends on it. The meadow was here first, and we mean to keep it that way.
