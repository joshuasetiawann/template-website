Our story

Eleven rooms, one quiet street

Maison Olivetta began as a private house on the narrowest street in the olive quarter, built when the town still pressed its own oil two doors down. When we took it on, the staircase leaned, the shutters had seized, and the courtyard was a jungle — but the bones were honest, and the light was extraordinary.

We chose to keep eleven rooms where a developer would have fitted twenty. Each one is different, shaped by the old house rather than fighting it: a window seat here, a sloping ceiling there, a bath tucked under the eaves. Nothing matches on purpose, and everything belongs.

There is no reception desk and no minibar. Instead there is an honesty bar in the hall, a courtyard for breakfast, and a host who lives upstairs and treats arrivals like friends turning up for the weekend. The neighbourhood cats run the courtyard; we simply manage the rooms.

Small means we can pay attention. We know which room you slept badly in last time, where you take your coffee, and which of the local trattorias to send you to on a Tuesday. A boutique hotel, properly done, is mostly memory and good manners.

Heritage

An old house, kept honest

We restored Maison Olivetta with the town's own craftspeople, reusing the original shutters, lime-washed walls and terracotta floors wherever they could be saved. The building is part of the quarter's fabric, and we look after it as custodians rather than owners.
