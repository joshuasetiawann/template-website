Our story

Cheap sleep, great city, good people

The Tramline Hostel lives in the city's old tram depot, where the carriages were once rolled in for the night. The rails are still set into the floor of the common room, the roller doors still open onto the street, and the volume — happily — has not dropped much since the trams left.

We built it for people travelling on not very much. Pod bunks with real curtains, lockers that take a proper padlock, private rooms when you need a door that closes, and showers with actual water pressure. Cheap should not mean grim, and a hostel can be both kind to your wallet and good to wake up in.

The crew is the point. They run the free walking tour, know which night market is on, keep a guitar behind the desk and will happily talk you into joining the rooftop barbecue. Travelling alone here lasts about as long as it takes to drop your bag and come back down the stairs.

We keep prices honest and the doors open late. No curfew, no lockout, no surprise fees — just a clean bunk, a sociable bar, a kitchen you can actually cook in and a city right outside that the crew can't wait to send you into.

Sustainability

Light on the planet, easy on the wallet

A hostel is green almost by accident — shared rooms, shared kitchens, shared everything. We push it further with LED throughout, refill stations instead of plastic bottles, serious recycling and a bike-first culture that means most guests never take a taxi. Doing more with less is rather the whole idea.
