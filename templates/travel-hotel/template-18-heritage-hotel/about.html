Our story

Four centuries, carefully kept

The Ashbourne Manor has stood in its parkland for four hundred years, and its walls have seen rather more history than most. It began as a Tudor house, gained a Georgian face, and very nearly fell to ruin in the last century before being rescued, room by panelled room, and turned over to the quiet business of hospitality.

We restored it as custodians, not developers. The oak panelling was cleaned rather than stripped, the plaster ceilings repaired by hand, the walled gardens replanted from the original plans, and the great fireplaces lit once more. Every change asked the same question: would the house approve? When the answer was no, we found another way.

Modern comfort hides behind the history. Beneath the four-poster beds and the leaded windows runs a quietly contemporary hotel — gentle heating, deep baths, fast but invisible internet — all tucked discreetly out of sight so that nothing intrudes on the sense of staying in a great house that simply happens to take guests.

The grounds are half the pleasure. Forty acres of parkland, a walled kitchen garden that supplies the dining room, ancient cedars, a ha-ha and a lake, all kept as the house was always meant to be. Guests walk the same gravel, sit in the same library and warm themselves at the same fires as four centuries of those before them.

Heritage

Saving a house for the next century

The Ashbourne is a listed building, and we steward it accordingly. Restoration uses lime plaster, reclaimed oak and traditional craft; the gardens follow the historic planting; and a share of every stay funds the slow, constant conservation a four-hundred-year-old house demands. We are simply its keepers for a while.
