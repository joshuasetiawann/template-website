Our story

The wild, with no one else in sight

Acacia Plains Safaris operates on a private conservancy rather than a public park, and that single fact changes everything. There are no day-trippers, no queues of minibuses at a lion sighting, and no rule that keeps you on the road. The land is leased and protected in partnership with the community that owns it, and the only camp on it is ours.

We keep the camp deliberately small — a handful of tents under the acacias, so that the conservancy never carries more than a few dozen guests at once. It means the wildlife is relaxed, the sightings feel earned, and the night sounds belong to the bush rather than the generator.

Our guides are the heart of it. Most grew up on this land or beside it, trained for years to read it, and can tell you not just what an animal is but what it is about to do. Off-road and unhurried, they will sit with a leopard for an hour or follow wild dogs across country no public-park vehicle could.

Conservation pays for the wild here. A nightly conservancy fee from every guest funds the rangers, the anti-poaching teams and the community lease that keeps this land wild rather than fenced and farmed. You are not just visiting the bush; you are helping to keep it.

Sustainability

Wildlife that pays its way

The conservancy model only works if the wild is worth more standing than cleared. A nightly fee from every guest funds rangers, anti-poaching patrols and the community lease that keeps this land open to wildlife. Solar runs the camp, water is drawn carefully, and the tents leave nothing behind when the season ends.
