Our collective

A school that lives behind the lens

Aperture Collective began as a few photographers sharing a studio and a belief: that photography is learned by shooting, with mentors, on real locations — never from a manual alone.

Every workshop is hands-on and small, capped at eight so each photographer gets real time with the mentor and the light. We shoot on location and in our daylight studio, and you leave each session with images you actually made.

We teach the eye before the gear. Light, composition, and intent come first; settings serve them. That’s why our students’ work transforms so quickly — and why a phone in skilled hands beats a pro camera in clueless ones.

Beyond workshops, our membership keeps the collective alive: monthly photo walks, studio access, honest critiques, and a community that keeps you shooting between courses.

Twice a year the whole collective exhibits. Every member — beginner to pro — prints and hangs a piece, and the neighbourhood comes to see the work made within these walls.

“Aperture taught me to see. The camera was never the problem — my eye was, and they fixed that.”

— Jordan Bell, Collective member

The Aperture path

From auto mode to a real portfolio

However you start, here’s the journey our photographers travel. Open any stage to see what you’ll shoot.

Before any settings, you learn to read light — its direction, quality, and colour. The single most important skill in photography, practised on day one.

The exposure triangle — aperture, shutter, ISO — drilled until manual mode is second nature and you control the image, not the camera.

Framing, balance, and leading the eye, so every photograph is a deliberate choice rather than a happy accident.

Directing portraits, finding candid moments, and the human side of making photographs people love.

Natural light, reflectors, and studio strobes — adding and controlling light to craft the image you imagined.

Working to a real creative brief on location, the way professional shoots actually happen.

A full editing workflow that finishes the image — colour, tone, and the final crop — without overcooking it.

Curating a cohesive series around a theme — the foundation of a portfolio or first exhibition.

Honest, supportive group critiques that sharpen your eye faster than shooting alone ever could.

Exhibit in our seasonal collective show, printed and hung alongside fellow photographers.
