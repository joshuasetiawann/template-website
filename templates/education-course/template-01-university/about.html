Our story

How Whitford University began

Every part of Whitford University grew from a single conviction about how people learn best.

Whitford University was chartered in 1871 by teachers and tradespeople who believed a serious education should reach far beyond the privileged few. The college opened with forty students and a single stone hall above the Connecticut River, and its founding promise — that close mentorship and free inquiry change lives — still governs every decision we make.

Through two centuries the campus has grown to 240 wooded acres and six schools, yet the scale of learning has stayed deliberately human. Full professors still teach first-year seminars capped at fourteen students, and every undergraduate completes the liberal-arts core before specializing.

The twentieth century brought the sciences, the professional schools, and a research mission — but never at the expense of the classroom. More than half of our undergraduates co-author scholarship with faculty before they graduate, and the senior capstone thesis remains the keystone of every degree.

Today Whitford educates roughly 6,400 undergraduates from 62 countries, remains independent, non-profit, and need-blind for domestic applicants, and counts 84,000 living alumni who keep the promise of 1871 alive across generations.
