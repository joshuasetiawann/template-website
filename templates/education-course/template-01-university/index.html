Our story

A community of inquiry since 1871

Whitford was chartered to make a serious education available beyond the privileged few — and that founding promise still shapes everything we do.

Founded by a group of teachers and tradespeople who believed learning should serve the wider world, Whitford opened its doors with forty students and one stone hall. Today the campus spans 240 wooded acres, yet the founding conviction remains: that close mentorship and free inquiry change lives.

Our liberal-arts core asks every student to read widely, argue carefully, and write well before specializing. Around that core sit professional schools in business, engineering, education, and the health sciences.

Research is woven through the undergraduate experience: more than half of our students co-author work with faculty before they graduate, in labs, archives, studios, and the field.

A Whitford education does not end at commencement. Our alumni network of 84,000 graduates mentors current students, opens internships, and keeps the community of inquiry alive across generations.

The Whitford core

Four years, one coherent path

Whatever your major, the core curriculum builds the habits of a serious mind in sequence. Open any year to see what it asks of you.

Two writing-intensive seminars, a quantitative-reasoning course, and a first language or lab science. Every first-year joins a 14-student seminar led by a senior professor.

A year-long great-books sequence reading primary sources from Plato to Baldwin, anchored by weekly debate and a portfolio of argumentative essays.

Students complete distribution across the humanities, social sciences, natural sciences, and the arts, ensuring no graduate leaves narrow.

By the end of the spring term students declare a major and meet the faculty mentor who will advise their remaining years and capstone.

Major-specific methods courses plus the option of a study-abroad term at one of 40 partner universities on five continents.

Students join a faculty research group, a professional co-op, or a community-engaged project for academic credit.

A two-semester independent project — a thesis, performance, exhibition, or engineering build — defended before a faculty committee.

Every senior presents their capstone publicly at the spring symposium attended by faculty, families, and recruiters.

Dedicated advising for graduate school, fellowships, and careers, with a guaranteed alumni mentor in your field.

Graduates join the 84,000-strong Whitford network and retain library, career, and mentoring privileges for life.
